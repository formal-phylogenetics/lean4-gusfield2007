# Audit of Gusfield et al. 2007 and its formalization

Paper: D. Gusfield, V. Bansal, V. Bafna, Y. S. Song, *A decomposition theory for phylogenetic networks and incompatible
characters*, [J. Comput. Biol. 14 (2007), 1247–1272](https://doi.org/10.1089/cmb.2006.0137), read in the PMC author manuscript
([PMC2581772](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2581772/)), and, for its symbols, an [author's copy](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~yss/Pub/decomposition.pdf) on Yun Song's page. No author TeX exists. The audit was made against the plain text of the PMC page, and the symbols that text loses were read in the author's copy. The journal's text wasn't reachable: the findings are about the author manuscript, and Section 3 says where that matters. Line numbers are locators in that text, as [`docs/Gusfield2007/CONFIGURATION.md`](CONFIGURATION.md) defines them. The labels of the route check (`g:…` for the paper, `ext:…` for its sources) are this project's. Section, result and equation numbers are the paper's.

This report covers the paper with the sources that only it uses: Gusfield 1991; Gusfield, Eddhu, Langley 2004; Hudson, Kaplan 1985; Myers, Griffiths 2003; Song, Hein 2005. It also uses Semple, Steel 2003, whose theorem is proved in the library [lean4-phylogenetics](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics). The formalization was made in [lean4-tob](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-tob), a collection on the tree of blobs, and moved here; the findings keep the numbers they have there: E42–E54, E67, E76–E79, E85.

Status of the formalization:

- **Proved, by the paper's arguments** except for the departures of Section 7: Lemmas 1–5; Theorems 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 11; Corollaries 1, 2, 3; and the refutation of all four Full-Decomposition Optimality Conjectures.
- **Theorem 4 is false as printed, and Theorem 10 only under a literal reading.** Theorem 4 fails by explicit networks (E42, E43), and its true form is proved, in both directions. Theorem 10 is false if `R(M + S)` is read by line 385's definition: for single crossovers by a counterexample proved in Lean, on 14 rows and 8 sites; for multiple crossovers given one finite computation that Lean's kernel hasn't completed, so that this one theorem is conditional, and says so in its statement. The paper's own use of that symbol, at lines 525 and 560, is for a network with ancestral sequence `S`, which suggests a slip for `R_S(M)`, and with `R_S(M)` the theorem is true, and proved in every crossover model, without distinct columns (E51).
- **Also proved, from what the paper says in passing:** the sentences at lines 135, 156, 180, 299, 329, 381, 403, 411, 418, 420, 554, 559, 590 and 608 (lines 156, 180, 403 and 554 in the corrected forms of E46–E49), Footnote 2, and the numbers of Figures 5–10. Section 9 lists what remains.
- **Proved cited results**, in [`Gusfield2007/External/`](../../Gusfield2007/External): the Perfect Phylogeny Theorem (Gusfield 1991), the Splits Equivalence Theorem (Semple, Steel 2003), Gusfield et al. 2004 (Theorems 2.2, 2.4–2.7, 8.1–8.3, Corollary 5.1 on galled trees, and an algorithm, proved correct, with a count of its operations), Myers and Griffiths 2003 (the haplotype bound, the integer program, Algorithms 1 and 3, Proposition 2, Corollary 5), Hudson and Kaplan 1985 (the bound and the algorithm of its Appendix 2), and Song and Hein 2005 (the bound by ordered SPR, and that the minimum over sequences of trees is `R¹`). - **Unconditional, apart from one labelled theorem.** No result of the literature is assumed. One theorem has a computation as a hypothesis: [`Gusfield2007.theorem10Printed_multiple_false_of_search`](../../Gusfield2007/Theorem10PrintedMultiple.lean#L294) assumes [`Gusfield2007.printedMSearch`](../../Gusfield2007/Theorem10PrintedMultiple.lean#L269), a finite search that the kernel hasn't completed. ## 1. Summary

- **Errors.** Theorem 4 is false as printed, because the definition of "efficient" omits the node where a blob is entered (E42) and because it doesn't exclude the site-free arcs that line 97 allows (E43); it holds once these are repaired, up to two conventions (E44). The first repair is in print: Gusfield's 2014 book *ReCombinatorics* counts that node, in a stricter definition, and states the theorem with it, though in the snippets we could read it doesn't say that the 2007 definition fails (E42). Theorem 10 is false if `R(M + S)` is read by line 385's definition: for single crossovers by a counterexample in Lean, and for multiple crossovers given one search that the kernel hasn't completed. The paper's own use of the symbol at lines 525 and 560 suggests a slip for `R_S(M)`, and with that it is true and proved (E51). Also false as printed: the characterization of the most tree-like network at line 373 (E45, E67), line 554 on galled networks, probably `M` for `M + S` as at line 552 (E49), line 560 on visible nodes, which needs distinct columns (E50), the step at line 403 on one `M` (E48), and line 180's wording of "reduced" (E47). None of these touches Theorem 1 or the refutation of the conjectures. The misprints are E54, all in both the PMC text and the author's copy.
- **Gaps.** E52, the modification of line 329, which the paper doesn't give, and E53, gaps in the proofs at lines 135, 141, 187–190, 264, 271, 295, 299, 312–316, 411, 418 and 576. All are repaired, and all the results hold.
- **Missing hypotheses.** E42–E51 and E67: Theorem 4 (E42–E44), line 373 (E45, E67), line 156 (E46), line 180 (E47), line 403 (E48), line 554 (E49), line 560 (E50) and Theorem 10 (E51). E43 and E46 are a case line 97 allows and the statements must exclude, and E44 two implicit conventions. Section 4 has all of them, with the paper's standing assumptions.
- **Redundant hypotheses.** Section 5: no constant column, in Theorem 4, and in Theorem 1, whose Lean statement keeps it; labels compared over all the blobs in "efficient", where the proof of Theorem 4 compares them on one blob; and, in the sources, binary trees in Song and Hein's bound, and single crossovers and rows at the leaves in the one-cycle fact of Gusfield et al. 2004.
- **Use of cited results.** The paper restates the definition of a reduced galled tree too weakly (E47), and applies Theorem 4 where it doesn't hold (line 418, E53). It applies the Perfect Phylogeny Theorem loosely (line 264, E53), and draws from the haplotype bound an equality case that needs distinct columns (E50). Its sentence on the galled networks of Huson and Klöpper 2007 is false as printed and true with `M + S` (E49). Section 2 lists gaps in the proofs of cited results. Among results only cited, one direction of Corollary 2.1 of Gusfield et al. 2004 is false, though that source's theorems hold (E76). Section 3 groups the points on cited works in E76–E79 and E85.

## 3. Errors and gaps in the paper

"Line N" here is a line of the plain text of the PMC author manuscript, one line per paragraph ([`docs/Gusfield2007/CONFIGURATION.md`](CONFIGURATION.md)). That plain text loses the symbols the page renders as images. An [author's copy on Yun Song's page](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~yss/Pub/decomposition.pdf), made from a DVI file on 2008-01-19, has the symbols and the same words apart from moved floats, footnotes and hyphenation, so it was used to check them. E49, E51 and E54 turn on symbols, and each is in that copy too, so none is an artifact of the PMC text. That copy isn't the journal's typeset text, which wasn't reachable, so whether the journal has them is unknown. The matrices and networks of the figures were compared with the PMC manuscript's images.

Neither Theorem 1, the paper's main result, nor the refutation of the conjectures is affected by anything here. Theorems 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 11 and Corollaries 1, 2, 3 hold as printed, under the paper's standing assumption that no column is constant. What fails is Theorem 4 as printed, Theorem 10 if `R(M + S)` is read by line 385's definition (E51), and some sentences around them.

**E42. Theorem 4: the definition of "efficient" omits the node where a blob is entered (an error).** Lines 326–328: "we say that a node v in blob b is an external node if there is an edge from v to some node outside of b. We say a phylogenetic network is efficient if [it] does not contain two external nodes with the same node labels, and for each external node v (in a blob b), there is exactly one edge from v to a node off of b." Theorem 4 (lines 334–336): for "any efficient fully-decomposed network for M", contracting the blobs gives a tree that "is necessarily the tree T̄".

The definition says nothing about the edge entering a blob, and neither does the proof. An exit can sit at the entry node of a blob while rows lie above the blob. Then line 342, "in the other subnetwork, no leaf label contains S_v(C)", is false: the rows above the blob contain the super-character of its top. Line 341, "each super-character derived from C labels exactly one external node", needs the entry node too: in Figure 2 made efficient, the super-character `00` of the component `{2, 5}` labels no external node ([`Gusfield2007.line341_false_fig2`](../../Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L2080)). There `T′` is `T̄`, so that one is a gap in the proof, not a further failure of the theorem.

The counterexample is [`Gusfield2007.topRows`](../../Gusfield2007/Examples.lean#L100), with rows `00000, 01000, 10000, 11001, 11011, 11101, 11111`. The network has a blob on `00000, 01000, 10000, 11000` with the sites 1, 2; an arc with the site 5 from `11000` to `11001`; a blob on `11001, 11011, 11101, 11111` with the sites 3, 4; and a pendant arc at each node of the two blobs other than `11000`. It is efficient in the printed sense and fully decomposed, in any crossover model, with no recombination leaf, and with a site on every arc that has both ends off the blobs and isn't pendant. The split `{00000, 01000, 10000, 11001} | {11011, 11101, 11111}` of `T̄` is missing from `T′`: [`Gusfield2007.theorem4_false_top`](../../Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L1943). The split is the witness to quote, since the number of edges depends on how line 264 is read. Moving the row `11001` up to a new node on the entering arc gives another efficient fully decomposed network, whose `T′` is `T̄`, so `T′` isn't invariant, against line 346: [`Gusfield2007.topRows_not_invariant`](../../Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L1952). A smaller instance, with one blob and a root of degree 3, is on `{0000, 0001, 0010, 0110, 1010, 1110}`: [`Gusfield2007.theorem4_false_small`](../../Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L1974).

The result stands in the following form. With the entry node counted, the theorem holds up to the two conventions of E44 and the site-free arcs of E43: for a fully decomposed network, `T′` is `T̄` exactly when the network has [`Gusfield2007.Network.IsEfficientAtEntry`](../../Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L1721), [`Gusfield2007.Network.RegularArcs`](../../Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L456) and [`Gusfield2007.Network.DegreeCond`](../../Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L467) ([`Gusfield2007.theorem4_entry`](../../Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L1843)). That definition reads "external" as having an edge to or from a node outside the blob, compares labels on one blob at a time, and counts the entering edge among the edges of its node. Lines 341 and 342 are then true: [`Gusfield2007.Network.IsEfficientAtEntry.existsUnique_port`](../../Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L1803), [`Gusfield2007.Network.IsEfficientAtEntry.line342Claim`](../../Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L1798). With the printed definition, [`Gusfield2007.theorem4_iff`](../../Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L1699) is exact: `T′` is `T̄` exactly when the network is efficient and has [`Gusfield2007.Network.IsTreeNormalStructural`](../../Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L888) and the degree condition. [`Gusfield2007.theorem4`](../../Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L1310) and [`Gusfield2007.theorem4_splits`](../../Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L929) are its forward direction. These hypotheses can be met: for every `M` with at least three rows there is such a network, with an interior root ([`Gusfield2007.exists_realization`](../../Gusfield2007/Realizability.lean#L327)). So `T̄` is the invariant structure of the networks the paper has in mind, and we read the omission as a slip in a definition: the proof's own line 341 needs the entry node. The use of Theorem 4 at line 418 is E53.

This repair is in print, and it isn't ours. Gusfield's book *ReCombinatorics* (MIT Press, 2014), Section 7.3.5, says "A node v in a blob B is an external node of B if v is adjacent (without regard to edge direction) to some node v′ not in B". It calls an ARG canonical if "No blob contains two nodes labeled with the same sequence", "Each external node v (in a blob B) is incident with exactly one cut edge", "If the root node v of a blob B is an external node, then the cut edge incident with v is directed into v", and "Every blob contains at least one incompatible pair of sites". Its Theorem 7.3.3 says "All canonical, fully decomposed ARGs for M have the same undirected backbone tree", and its Corollary 7.3.4 is line 341 repaired: the number of external nodes of a blob "is exactly the number of distinct sequences in M(C)". We read the book only through short snippets from Open Library's public search inside, and its table of contents, and not its proofs; someone should read Sections 7.3.5 and 7.4 in print. The snippets show no statement that the 2007 definition fails, and the section's introduction says "(with some technical qualification)". What the Lean adds is the failure under the printed definition, by explicit networks ([`Gusfield2007.theorem4_false_top`](../../Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L1943), [`Gusfield2007.topRows_not_invariant`](../../Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L1952)); the exact characterization in both directions, where the snippets show one direction, for `IsEfficientAtEntry`, which is weaker than canonical (a canonical ARG is efficient at the entry, but not conversely: a blob can repeat a label away from its external nodes, and [`Gusfield2007.fourRootNet`](../../Gusfield2007/CounterexampleNets.lean#L439) has an exit at the root of its blob, against the third condition); and the check in the kernel.

A network deriving all the rows can't have `T′ = T̄` with its root at a row ([`Gusfield2007.root_not_mem_range`](../../Gusfield2007/RootedFrame.lean#L1028)), so there the row has no leaf ([`Gusfield2007.exists_network_rooted_at_row`](../../Gusfield2007/RootedFrame.lean#L994)).
- **Lines 312–316, Theorem 3.** "Establishes" (line 312) omits three steps: if `S(C) ∈ M(C)`, a leaf labelled `S(C)` has no edge of `T̄` to carry ([`Gusfield2007.RS_erase_eq`](../../Gusfield2007/Decomposition.lean#L300)); an optimal network has no site-free blob ([`Gusfield2007.Decomp.exists_copy_of_siteFree`](../../Gusfield2007/DecompNet.lean#L462)); and if `S ∈ M`, the row needs its own leaf below the root ([`Gusfield2007.Decomp.addLeaf`](../../Gusfield2007/DecompNet.lean#L241)).
- **Lines 341–342.** See E42.
- **Line 411.** That every `M` is min decomposable for recombinations modelling mutations "follows from results on the maximum parsimony problem using median networks, as established by Bandelt et al. (1995)". Those results are there: "all most parsimonious trees for a set V of 0-1 vectors can be realized in the median network" (p. 745), and at a cut node of that network the characters split into two sets, and the most parsimonious trees "are exactly the trees composed by the MP trees" of the two parts (p. 747; "decomposition along cut nodes", p. 748). Neither paper writes the link from cut nodes to the components of `G(M)`, which the statement needs; for Buneman graphs it appeared later, as Theorem 5.1 of Dress et al. 2011 (Section 10). The statement holds: [`Gusfield2007.minDecomposable_pointwise`](../../Gusfield2007/SufficientConditions.lean#L168), by another route, which isn't forced (Section 7).
- **Line 418.** "The correctness of the procedure follows from Theorem 4 and the fact that placing the root of T̄ at a leaf or on the interior of an edge will not reduce the number of required recombinations". Theorem 4 is false as printed, and an optimal fully decomposed network needn't satisfy its corrected form. The conclusion holds: [`Gusfield2007.F_eq_sInf_interior`](../../Gusfield2007/Conjectures.lean#L696), and the second fact is [`Gusfield2007.exists_interior_sum_rootCost_le`](../../Gusfield2007/Conjectures.lean#L651) (Section 7).
- **Line 576.** The two conditions of the modification don't exclude a recombination node labelled `S`, and the count is then off by one ([`Gusfield2007.exists_reattach_of_card_lt`](../../Gusfield2007/SufficientConditions.lean#L712)). ## 6. How the formalization reads the paper

- **Sites and rows.** Sites are a linearly ordered type, a row is a function to `Bool`, and `M` is a finite set of rows, since the paper assumes no duplicate rows (line 112). `M(C)` is the set of distinct restrictions, as the super-characters of line 245 are. The standing assumption that no column is constant is [`Gusfield2007.NoConstantColumn`](../../Gusfield2007/Defs.lean#L74), a hypothesis of the results that use it.
- **Networks** ([`Gusfield2007.Network`](../../Gusfield2007/Defs.lean#L129)) have no parallel arcs. [`Gusfield2007.Network.Derives`](../../Gusfield2007/Defs.lean#L180) is a bijection from the leaves to the rows, since the network has "exactly n" leaves. A recombination node may be a leaf, as nothing at line 94 excludes it; this is what E44 is about. There is no bridge from these networks, which carry sites and sequences, to the rooted networks of the other papers of the collection, and the modules reach only Mathlib and their sources in [`Gusfield2007/External/`](../../Gusfield2007/External).
- **Crossovers.** A single crossover is a prefix of one parent followed by a suffix of the other, the prefix being a lower set, possibly empty or everything; a recombination that copies a parent can be dropped, so no minimum changes. `Gusfield2007.Crossover.pointwise` is a third model, the recombinations of Section 4.1 that model one back or recurrent mutation, which Corollary 3 needs: "S-min decomposable" can't mean multiple crossovers there, since Figure 8 refutes that.
- **Blobs.** A blob is the arc set of a class of recombination cycles under sharing an arc. "No blob shares a node with any other blob" (lines 135, 140) is [`Gusfield2007.Network.BlobsNodeDisjoint`](../../Gusfield2007/Defs.lean#L224), a conjunct of [`Gusfield2007.Network.IsFullyDecomposedFor`](../../Gusfield2007/Defs.lean#L257); [`Gusfield2007.Network.exists_blobsNodeDisjoint`](../../Gusfield2007/Blobs.lean#L1180) shows that no count depends on the convention. `T′` is the quotient of the nodes by the components of the arcs on cycles, a tree with no assumption on blobs ([`Gusfield2007.Network.blobbedTree_isTree`](../../Gusfield2007/Blobs.lean#L465)).
- **`T̄`** ([`Gusfield2007.IsTBar`](../../Gusfield2007/Defs.lean#L433)) has every row at a leaf, every internal edge labelled, and no node of degree two, as lines 271–289 use it. Line 264's "each edge is labeled" can't hold with rows at leaves. It agrees with the split families of the library, which contain the singletons. With line 264's reading, `T̄` of [`topRows`](../../Gusfield2007/Examples.lean#L100) has eight edges, which is why E42 quotes the split.
- **Theorem 4's form.** [`Gusfield2007.Theorem4Form`](../../Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L49) asks for an isomorphism of graphs from `T′` to `T̄` that respects the rows at the leaves, which is what "is necessarily the tree" means.
- **`R`, `F`.** [`Gusfield2007.R`](../../Gusfield2007/Defs.lean#L291), [`Gusfield2007.RS`](../../Gusfield2007/Defs.lean#L295), [`Gusfield2007.F`](../../Gusfield2007/Defs.lean#L300), [`Gusfield2007.FS`](../../Gusfield2007/Defs.lean#L305) are minima over networks on finite types, in `ℕ`. `sInf ∅ = 0` occurs only on an empty `M`, which is excluded. The haplotype bound [`Gusfield2007.haplotypeBound`](../../Gusfield2007/Defs.lean#L114) is an integer, as printed, and is negative on `{10, 01}`. `d_oSPR` is in `ℕ∞`, as unreachable pairs would otherwise cost 0.

They say the blocks "are also closely related to the 'blobs' defined in terms of directed graphs described by D. Gusfield et al.". This is the route by Buneman graphs that line 350 mentions. Read side by side, their reduced tree `T_Σ` looks like `T̄`; neither paper says so, and we didn't check it further. Lam, Gusfield, Sridhar, [SIAM J. Discrete Math. 25 (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1137/090776305), generalize the Splits Equivalence Theorem and the four-gamete condition to three-state characters; title and abstract only. [Allman et al. 2023](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-tob/blob/main/papers/AllmanEtAl2023/README.md) cite this paper for the tree of blobs, and the later papers of the collection take it from there or from this paper. We found no later study that repeats the empirical claim of Table 1.

## 11. What's next

### Open directions

- **How much does full decomposition cost?** Theorem 11 says `F¹ − R¹` is unbounded for single crossovers. Line 666 says "we do not know how robust these empirical results are in other ranges of data, or to multiple-crossover recombination, or when there is a fixed ancestral sequence". We found nothing on this in the works of Section 10, and Gusfield's 2014 book, whose Section 7.4.3 and Chapter 11 are about this, should be read first.
- **[`Gusfield2007.MinDecomposableOfForallS`](../../Gusfield2007/Conjectures.lean#L118).** Line 425's "when M is S-min decomposable for all S, then M is min-decomposable" is unproved here. [`Gusfield2007.fig6_hG_necessary`](../../Gusfield2007/Counterexamples.lean#L252) shows why it can't be got one root at a time.
- **`T̄` and the tree of blobs.** A remark that is unverified: on paper, not in Lean, and read by no independent checker. For a binary network, `T̄` of all the splits of its displayed trees should be its tree of blobs, at any level. A split `A|B` of `T̄` is compatible with every displayed split, so every displayed tree shows `a₁a₂|b₁b₂` across it, so by [`AllmanEtAl2023.InPassing.isTreelike_iff_forall_displays`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-tob/blob/47250e1/src/AllmanEtAl2023/InPassing.lean#L513) and [`ECToBlob.theorem4_1`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-tob/blob/47250e1/src/ECToBlob/Section4.lean#L55) (or Huber et al.'s Theorem 5.1) it is a cut-edge split. Conversely a cut-edge split is in every displayed tree and is a trivial component. A script tried it on 3,000 random binary networks with at most four reticulations; the script isn't kept in the repository. Under the displayed tree model `T̄` would then be an exact estimator. Under the coalescent, where every pair of gene-tree splits is eventually incompatible, is anything left of this?

### Simpler proofs

Each item says whether it was checked in Lean. We found none of them published, in the search of Section 10.

- **Gusfield et al., the lower bounds of Figure 8.** The paper runs a program to show that a component needs two multiple-crossover recombinations, since one recombination node is a galled tree. A shorter argument: with one recombination node `x`, the incompatibility graph is bipartite. Colour a site on one side of the cycle by its state at `x`, and a site on the other side by the opposite: two incompatible sites then get different colours, and sites off the cycle are compatible with all. So a triangle of incompatible sites forces two recombination nodes, and each component of Figure 8 has one, as do those of [`Gusfield2007.evenRows`](../../Gusfield2007/Examples.lean#L81). Checked on paper, and the triangles by script; the formal proof uses a verified search ([`Gusfield2007.LabelSearch.lt_of_check`](../../Gusfield2007/LabelSearch.lean#L277)).
- **Gusfield et al., line 411.** Re-rooting at a row of an optimal tree, and Corollary 3, give that every `M` is min decomposable for recombinations that model mutations, with no median network ([`Gusfield2007.minDecomposable_pointwise`](../../Gusfield2007/SufficientConditions.lean#L168)). Checked in Lean. It isn't the route line 411 points to, which stays to be built (below).
- **Gusfield et al., existence of `T̄`.** The Splits Equivalence Theorem applied to the character splits with the singleton splits gives `T̄` directly, with no contraction of the Perfect Phylogeny Theorem's tree. A sketch; the formal proof contracts, as line 264 suggests ([`Gusfield2007.IsPerfectPhylogeny.exists_normal`](../../Gusfield2007/TreeContract.lean#L321)).
- **Gusfield et al. 2004, Theorem 2.2, in the form 2007 cites.** No contraction or deletion: if no recombination node breaks `{i, j}`, only three pairs occur; if one does, its parents differ at both sites, and tracing states up gives a cycle through each site's arc and both arcs into that node, and these cycles share an arc. This avoids the source's unproved claim that an arc can be deleted. It is the Lean proof, [`External.GusfieldEtAl2004.exists_isBlob_of_incompatible`](../../Gusfield2007/External/GusfieldEtAl2004/SameBlob.lean#L124).
- **Reduced galled trees.** Lemma 2.3 and Theorems 2.4, 2.5 of the source go by the arrays `W1, W2, W3`, one root and single crossovers. A threshold on a line through the gall's nodes covers the three arrays at once, for any root and crossover model ([`External.GusfieldEtAl2004.exists_threshold`](../../Gusfield2007/External/GusfieldEtAl2004/GalledTree.lean#L397)). It is the Lean proof.

### The formalization

Nothing from the literature is assumed (Section 2), and what isn't formalized is in Section 9. Beyond those:

- **Reading the 2014 book.** [`Gusfield2007.theorem4_entry`](../../Gusfield2007/Uniqueness.lean#L1843) and the book's Theorem 7.3.3 should be compared in print, with the book's definition of "most treelike" against E45, and whether its ARGs allow the arcs of E43. Its proof of Theorem 7.3.2 should be compared with [`Gusfield2007.exists_isEfficientAtEntry_of_fullyDecomposed`](../../Gusfield2007/EntryNet.lean#L516).
- **[`Gusfield2007.printedMSearch`](../../Gusfield2007/Theorem10PrintedMultiple.lean#L269).** The kernel hasn't completed this computation, so the multiple-crossover case of E51 stays conditional until it does, or until another proof of the same bound exists.
- **`T̄` and the Buneman graph.** A proof that `T_Σ` of Dress et al. is `T̄` would connect [`Gusfield2007/`](../../Gusfield2007) to a published canonical construction, and would give another proof of Theorem 1 and of [`Gusfield2007.isTBar_unique`](../../Gusfield2007/SuperCharacters.lean#L264).
- **Line 411 of Gusfield et al. by cut nodes.** [`Gusfield2007.minDecomposable_pointwise`](../../Gusfield2007/SufficientConditions.lean#L168) goes by re-rooting and Corollary 3, which isn't the route line 411 points to (Section 7). That route would build the median network of `M`, with its retract property (which Bandelt et al. cite from Bandelt 1984 and van de Vel 1993), and Bandelt et al.'s decomposition at cut nodes, and would add what neither paper writes: that the cut nodes separate the components of `G(M)`. Another route, also unbuilt, goes through Theorem 9: a majority of three rows creates no new incompatible pair, so that labels in the median network respect `G(M)`. Both are sketches. Neither has been built, because of their cost.
- **A bridge between the two kinds of network.** [`Gusfield2007.Network`](../../Gusfield2007/Defs.lean#L129) carries sites and sequences, and the library's [`Phylogenetics.RootedNetwork`](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics/blob/b56ce531644230622f069aaa8aa51a5143c1411e/Phylogenetics/Network/Defs.lean#L30) carries lengths and inheritance, and nothing connects them. The blobs and trees of blobs of the two could be compared, by forgetting the labels. [`Gusfield2007`](../../Gusfield2007) reaches only Mathlib, its sources and the library's Splits Equivalence Theorem, so this would be a new module.
- **What the unit holds for reuse.** It imports Mathlib, and from the library [lean4-phylogenetics](https://github.com/vltanh/lean4-phylogenetics) only the Splits Equivalence Theorem of Semple, Steel 2003, whose modules import Mathlib only.
